ONE MUST FALL: 2097 GUIDE
=========================

Pilots fight in giant robots (HARs) for the WAR corporation. Freeware
since 1999.

Getting it onto the stick
-------------------------
  Save the freeware zip from omf2097.com into the builder's downloads
  folder and run the builder again. Run SETUP once for sound.

The robots (HARs)
-----------------
  Jaguar     fast all-rounder with a leaping grab. Best first pick.
  Shadow     uses shadow copies of itself; tricky
  Thorn      spikes and a flying kick
  Pyros      jets of fire, flies briefly
  Electra    electric balls and a rolling charge
  Katana     blade uppercuts, rising attacks
  Shredder   flying head-butts and claws
  Flail      swinging chains, long reach
  Gargoyle   flies, dives, claws
  Chronos    stasis and teleporting
  Nova       the heavy: missiles and mines

How to fight
------------
  * Hold back (away from the opponent) to block.
  * Special moves are joystick-style motions + punch or kick, like other
    fighting games: down, down-forward, forward + punch for example.
    The in-game Help and the README list each robot's moves.
  * When the opponent is dazed at the end of a round, a "scrap" or
    "destruction" finishing move is possible; the listed moves in the
    manual show them.
  * Arenas have hazards (the power plant, the fire pit, the spikes):
    throw opponents into them.

Tournament mode
---------------
  Your pilot earns money per fight. Spend it on:
    Arm power / Leg power   more damage
    Arm speed / Leg speed   faster attacks
    Armor                   less damage taken
    Stun resistance         get dizzy later
  Buy upgrades before a new robot; a fully trained cheap robot beats an
  untrained expensive one. Save between tournament rounds.

Tips
----
  * Start in 1 Player with Jaguar on the easiest difficulty and learn
    the jump-in kick, sweep and throw first.
  * Most special moves have recovery time: block them, then punish.
  * Two players on one keyboard works: each side's keys are in SETUP.

The open-source OpenOMF project also runs this data on modern systems.
